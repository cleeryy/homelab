Last synced with live infrastructure: **September 2026**.

Two machines, two personalities. `pve1` is the old workhorse — modest, lightly loaded, carrying the legacy VMs nobody has had a reason to move. `pve3` is where everything happens now: twice the threads, nearly triple the RAM, and the load average to prove it, hosting the firewall, the platform services, and the media stack all at once. That imbalance is intentional but worth watching; if `pve3` ever looks full, the answer is not "add a third node" but "finish moving the legacy VMs off `pve1`'s shadow".

## Active nodes

Both nodes run the same Proxmox VE release and share the same management and cluster networks, which is what keeps the two-node cluster quorate despite their very different hardware generations. The table below is the addressing contract everything else builds on: management IPs on the flat LAN, service IPs on `vlan50`, and a common gateway. Individual hardware, bridges, and load come right after, per node, so this table stays the quick lookup while the prose carries the interpretation.

| Node | Role | Proxmox VE | Management | Gateway |
|------|------|-----------|------------|---------|
| `pve1` | Infrastructure and management | 9.2.11 (kernel `7.0.14-8-pve`) | `192.168.1.101/24` on `vmbr0` + `10.1.50.101` (`vlan50`) | `192.168.1.1` |
| `pve3` | Primary compute and production | 9.2.11 (kernel `7.0.14-15-pve`) | `192.168.1.103/24` on `vmbr0` + `10.1.50.103` (`vlan50`) | `192.168.1.1` |

The shared gateway and parallel `vlan50` addresses mean either host can reach the management VLAN and the flat LAN without transiting the other. That symmetry matters during maintenance, when one node may be rebooted while the other still serves DNS, backups, and ingress.

### `pve1`

The `pve1` host is a dual Xeon E5-2450 v2 machine at 2.50 GHz with 32 threads and 64 GB of RAM, booting in legacy BIOS mode. Its networking pairs a management bridge on the LAN with a VLAN-aware trunk toward `pve3` covering VLANs 2 through 4094, plus a `vlan50` interface at `10.1.50.101/24` for management traffic. Storage spans local directories and LVM alongside a local ZFS pool named `pve1-storage` and the shared NFS mounts `nas` and `truenas` plus the `pbs` backup target. In practice the node hosts legacy application VMs running Docker stacks together with the code and database containers and a handful of utilities. It was lightly loaded at last observation, with load average near 2.7, roughly 37 GB of RAM in use, and about fifteen days of uptime.

### `pve3`

The `pve3` host is the larger machine, with two Xeon Silver 4208 processors at 2.10 GHz for 32 threads and roughly 172 GiB of RAM, booting via EFI. Its `vmbr0` carries the management LAN while `vmbr1` forms the trunk toward `pve1` as a VLAN-aware bridge carrying the OPNsense tags 10, 20, 25, 30, 40, 50, and 60, complemented by `vlan50` at `10.1.50.103/24` and a persistent static route for `10.1.0.0/16` via `10.1.50.1` so the host itself can reach the VLANs. Storage leans on a large local LVM-thin pool with the shared `nas` and `truenas` NFS mounts and the `pbs` backup target, since there is no active host-side ZFS pool; the ZFS layer on this node belongs to the TrueNAS VM through passed-through physical disks. That concentration shows in the load figures, with load average observed between 34 and 51, about 103 GB of RAM in use plus a gigabyte of swap, and under four days of uptime at last check.

## Removed

Retired nodes are recorded here rather than silently dropped so that old references to them resolve to an explanation. The cluster previously included a third member that has since been fully ejected, leaving no workloads and no storage entries behind, which is why current architecture and inventory pages speak only of `pve1` and `pve3`.

| Node | Status | Notes |
|------|--------|-------|
| `pve2` | Removed | Ejected from the cluster; no workloads, no storage entry. |

No migration is pending for this entry and no disks or pools still point at it. If a stale `pve2` reference appears in logs or configuration, it is historical rather than a missing host.

## Known gaps

The software side of these nodes is fully captured, from Proxmox versions down to bridge VLAN lists, but the physical metal is not yet fully described. Which disk sits in which slot, which devices are passed through to TrueNAS, and the BIOS and firmware levels would complete the picture. Those are the facts nobody misses until a drive fails at 1 AM, so closing this gap before that night arrives would pay for itself immediately.
