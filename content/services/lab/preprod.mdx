Last synced with live infrastructure: **September 2026**.

Preproduction is where experiments live before they earn the right to touch real data. The deal is simple: anything here may break, may be wiped, and must never be depended on by a production service. The preproduction VLAN exists precisely to make that promise enforceable at the network layer, even though today only one guest actually lives on it. If you are about to test something risky, this is where it goes — not on the production VLAN next to Immich and Nextcloud.

The table above is the whole fleet: one running project host and two stopped ones awaiting a decision. `projet-paul` is the only guest currently living on the preproduction VLAN, which otherwise sits idle, while the two stopped ESGI hosts hold disk and IDs but no running workload. The old `lockbits` preproduction and production containers (`310`, `320`) have been deleted and are noted here only so nobody goes looking for them.

## Purpose

These are project-specific hosts outside the production data path. They exist so experiments have somewhere to run that is neither the production VLAN nor somebody's laptop, and they earn detailed pages only when they graduate toward production — until then, a paragraph each is the right size.

## Placement

All pre-production workloads live on `pve3`, separated from the production stacks by VLAN for `257` and by convention for the stopped hosts.

## Operational notes

These are lab workloads, not production services: nothing user-facing should rely on them, and their state can change without notice. Backup posture is the general PBS job with best-effort per-guest restore priority — enough to recover an experiment, not a promise to preserve one.

## Known gaps

Functional descriptions for both project hosts, plus their reachability and exposure model, remain undocumented. Lab workloads earn detail only as they approach production; until then the gap is intentional.
