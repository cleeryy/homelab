Last synced with live infrastructure: **September 2026**.

This is the box that decides whether an outage is a bad evening or a catastrophe. Every scheduled backup in the homelab lands here, on a dedicated datastore, and from here an encrypted copy leaves for Google Drive every night. The design accepts an uncomfortable truth — PBS shares a physical node with most of what it protects — and compensates with that off-site copy plus mandatory restore drills. The rule written on the architecture page bears repeating: a backup that has never been restore-tested does not exist.

## Off-site

Encrypted rclone `crypt` sync to Google Drive (`gdrive-crypt:backupstore` + `pbs-config`), daily 06:00 from CT `578` under `flock`. Never run two syncs in parallel.

## Maintenance and proof

- Weekly garbage collection + monthly verify via the PBS UI.
- Last restore drill booted successfully on **10/09/2026**. Re-drill after any major change — an untested backup does not exist.

## Admin UI

`pbs.home.cleeryy.cloud` → Caddy → `https://pbs:8007` (private zone).

## Failure impact

- PBS down → scheduled backups stop; existing backups remain on the datastore but are not taken. The off-site copy continues to reflect the last successful sync.
- PBS shares a node (`pve3`) with most of what it protects — the encrypted off-site copy is the real disaster-recovery path.

## Known gaps

Datastore headroom is the number to watch: ~600 GB fills silently until jobs start failing, so a growth trend (even a monthly glance at the PBS dashboard) belongs in the routine. GC/verify history is similarly only visible inside the PBS UI — exporting it here can wait until someone automates it.
